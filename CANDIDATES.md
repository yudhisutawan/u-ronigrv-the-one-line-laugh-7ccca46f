# Candidates — head-to-head tournament

Judge: the brief published in full in the kit's rules (criteria in order):
1. **Funny line ≤200 chars** — a line that is not funny, or runs past the
   limit, loses to any funny line within 200 characters.
2. **Surprise at the end** — a real turn the reader did not see coming
   that still makes sense once it arrives.
3. **Everyday truth** — something true of ordinary life the reader nods at.
4. **Economy** — every word earns its place, nothing there to explain
   the joke.
5. **Freshness** — feels fresh rather than a line heard many times before.

Method: every pair judged on the criteria in order — the first criterion
where they differ decides, unless a later one differs by much more.
Margin 1 = by a hair, 5 = overwhelmingly. 9 candidates = 36 games.
All candidates are one sentence, 95–145 characters (limit 200).

## The candidates

- **A — self-checkout** (145 chars): "The self-checkout asked if I needed help, I said no, then spent five minutes arguing with a machine about an unexpected item in the bagging area."
- **B — inbox zero** (95 chars): "I finally reached inbox zero, and my inbox immediately sent me a congratulatory email about it."
- **C — screen time** (135 chars): "My phone sent me a weekly screen-time report, so I spent an hour staring at my phone to read how much time I spend staring at my phone."
- **D — group chat** (113 chars): "I muted the group chat to stop the noise, then spent twenty minutes reading all 300 messages I muted it to avoid."
- **E — password reset** (109 chars): "I reset my password to something brand new, and the site rejected it for being a password I have used before."
- **F — autocorrect** (108 chars): "Autocorrect changed 'I'll be there soon' to 'I'll be their spoon', and my boss just replied 'bring cutlery'."
- **G — to-do list** (133 chars): "I wrote 'stop procrastinating' at the top of my to-do list, then spent an hour color-coding the list instead of doing anything on it."
- **H — gym** (102 chars): "I pay forty dollars a month for a gym membership so my wallet gets the workout my body keeps skipping."
- **I — meta AI** (118 chars): "An AI spent an hour writing this one-line joke, so if you don't laugh, blame the machine that billed me by the minute."

## Winner: F — autocorrect (8–0, undefeated)

> Autocorrect changed 'I'll be there soon' to 'I'll be their spoon', and my boss just replied 'bring cutlery'.

Why it won, against the brief:
- **Surprise (2):** two turns, not one — the typo itself ("their spoon"),
  then the boss's deadpan reply ("bring cutlery"). The second voice is
  the extra beat no other candidate has.
- **Everyday truth (3):** autocorrect disasters are universal; everyone
  has sent a mangled text to exactly the wrong person.
- **Economy (4):** 108 characters, every word earns its place; the joke
  needs no setup beyond the quote.
- **Freshness (5):** autocorrect jokes are a genre, but this exact
  construction — spoon, then a boss who plays along — is original.

Runner-up E (7–1) lost only to F, on surprise: one paradox twist against
F's two beats. D (6–2) is the strongest fallback if F is ever judged
weaker than expected.
